# Taxonomy Guide – COMP 370 Homework 9

## 1. Motivation

This taxonomy was developed to classify the main topics discussed in the r/mcgill and r/concordia subreddits. Its purpose is to provide a consistent framework for comparing student discussions between the two universities.

The taxonomy contains eight categories based on the topics identified in Homework 8. This guide provides definitions, examples, and rules for handling ambiguous posts so that different annotators can apply the same classification criteria.

**General annotation rules:**
- Assign exactly one category to each post.
- Classify the post based on its main topic or purpose, rather than individual keywords.
- Use the post title and any available context.
- When multiple categories appear relevant, select the one that best represents the author's primary intention.
- Do not discuss individual annotations with other annotators.

## 2. Taxonomy Categories

### 1. Academic Support

**Definition:** Posts requesting or providing direct assistance with academic tasks, including assignments, studying, examinations, and course materials.

**Examples (illustrative):**
- Asking for help understanding an assignment question.
- Looking for study partners or course notes.

**Edge cases:**
- Include requests for immediate assistance with coursework.
- Exclude questions about which courses to take or how to plan a degree; classify these as Academic Advice.

### 2. Academic Advice

**Definition:** Posts seeking or sharing recommendations about academic decisions, course selection, degree planning, or educational pathways.

**Examples (illustrative):**
- Asking whether a particular course is difficult.
- Requesting advice about choosing a major or minor.

**Edge cases:**
- Include questions about course workload when the purpose is deciding whether to enroll.
- Exclude requests for help completing coursework; classify these as Academic Support.

### 3. Career and Employment

**Definition:** Posts discussing employment opportunities, internships, professional development, or career planning.

**Examples (illustrative):**
- Asking about internship opportunities.
- Seeking advice on finding part-time employment.

**Edge cases:**
- Include career-oriented questions about future employment.
- Exclude questions primarily about selecting academic courses unless career preparation is the central focus.

### 4. Student Life

**Definition:** Posts discussing students' everyday experiences, social activities, housing, relationships, or life outside academic coursework.

**Examples (illustrative):**
- Looking for friends or social activities.
- Discussing student housing experiences.

**Edge cases:**
- Include general discussions about living and socializing as a university student.
- Exclude posts primarily expressing dissatisfaction with university services; classify these as Complaint.

### 5. Complaint

**Definition:** Posts primarily expressing dissatisfaction, frustration, or criticism concerning university experiences, policies, facilities, or services.

**Examples (illustrative):**
- Complaining about crowded study spaces.
- Expressing frustration with university administration.

**Edge cases:**
- Include negative experiences when expressing dissatisfaction is the main purpose.
- Exclude neutral requests for information about a service; classify these as Campus Resources and Services.
- Exclude factual reporting of university policy changes without substantial criticism; classify these as News and University Affairs.

### 6. News and University Affairs

**Definition:** Posts sharing or discussing university-related news, announcements, administrative decisions, institutional policies, or significant events.

**Examples (illustrative):**
- Sharing an announcement about university policy changes.
- Discussing major developments involving university administration.

**Edge cases:**
- Include factual announcements about university-wide developments.
- Exclude posts primarily criticizing those developments; classify these as Complaint.

### 7. Campus Resources and Services

**Definition:** Posts requesting or providing practical information about university facilities, services, resources, or administrative procedures.

**Examples (illustrative):**
- Asking about library opening hours.
- Requesting information about campus facilities or student services.

**Edge cases:**
- Include questions about accessing or using university services.
- Exclude complaints about the quality of those services; classify these as Complaint.
- Exclude requests for direct assistance with academic assignments; classify these as Academic Support.

### 8. General Discussion and Humor

**Definition:** Posts involving casual conversation, jokes, memes, general observations, or discussions that do not fit the other categories.

**Examples (illustrative):**
- Sharing a university-related meme.
- Posting a humorous observation about campus culture.

**Edge cases:**
- Include humorous posts when entertainment is the primary purpose.
- Exclude serious complaints expressed with minor humor; classify these as Complaint.
- Use this category for general discussions that do not clearly belong to another category.

## 3. Handling Ambiguous Posts

When a post appears to fit multiple categories, annotators should identify its primary purpose.

For example:
- A student criticizing an examination while asking for study strategies should be classified according to whether the main purpose is expressing dissatisfaction or seeking academic help.
- A post asking how to access a university career service belongs to Campus Resources and Services, whereas a post asking how to obtain an internship belongs to Career and Employment.
- A humorous post primarily criticizing a university policy belongs to Complaint if criticism is the dominant purpose.

Annotators should apply these rules consistently and make their decisions independently.

## 4. Changes from Homework 8

The original eight categories have been retained. Definitions and boundary rules have been expanded to support consistent annotation by multiple people.

No category changes are proposed in this draft.
